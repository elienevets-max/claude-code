# Skill 3: Value Compression (High Signal Per Second)

## What It Is

Delivering clear, meaningful payoff to the viewer as quickly and efficiently as possible. Every second of content should carry signal — information, emotion, or identity resonance that the viewer registers as worthwhile.

## Core Principle

One video = one clear outcome. If a viewer finishes your video and can't immediately articulate what they gained, the value wasn't compressed enough. TikTok rewards density because dense content gets completed, rewatched, and saved.

## Types of Value

### Practical Utility
- Teach the viewer something they can use immediately
- "Here's how to..." / "Do this instead of..."
- The strongest utility content gives a specific action, not a general concept
- Test: Could the viewer do something differently after watching? If yes, it's practical utility

### Emotional Payoff
- Make the viewer feel something — surprise, relief, validation, humor, inspiration
- Emotional content doesn't need to teach; it needs to resonate
- The payoff should be felt, not explained
- Test: Does the viewer have a gut reaction? If yes, it's emotional payoff

### Identity Reinforcement
- Make the audience feel seen, understood, or represented
- "This is so me" content drives shares and saves
- Works by naming something the viewer has felt but hasn't articulated
- Test: Would the viewer tag someone or say "literally me"? If yes, it's identity reinforcement

## Operational Rules

### One Video, One Outcome
- Don't try to teach three things in one video — teach one thing well
- Multiple points dilute each other; the viewer remembers none of them clearly
- If you have three ideas, make three videos

### Cut Everything That Doesn't Serve the Outcome
- Every word, visual, and second either advances the single outcome or it's dead weight
- Common cuts: introductions, qualifications, tangents, repeated phrasing
- Ask of each sentence: "Does this help deliver the one outcome?"

### Front-Load Clarity
- The viewer should understand the value proposition within seconds
- Don't make them guess what the video is about
- Clear framing up front ("Here's the fastest way to...") lets the viewer settle in and absorb

### Specificity Over Generality
- "Post at 7 PM on Tuesday" > "Post at the right time"
- "Use a green screen with a screenshot" > "Try different formats"
- Specific = actionable = valuable
- General advice feels like filler even when it's technically correct

## Value Density Formula

**Value per second = (New information + Emotional impact + Relevance) / Time**

Maximize the numerator. Minimize the denominator. Never pad.

## Skill Benchmark

A viewer finishes the video and can state in one sentence what they got from it.

## Common Mistakes

- Trying to cover too much in one video (dilutes everything)
- Providing general advice instead of specific tactics (feels hollow)
- Adding "bonus tips" that weaken the core message
- Confusing length with depth (a 15-second video can be deeper than a 3-minute one)

## Practice Drill

After recording a video, write one sentence describing the viewer's takeaway. If you can't do it clearly, the video lacks compression. Re-edit until the takeaway is unmistakable.
