# Skill 4: Algorithm Signaling Literacy

## What It Is

Creating content that generates the specific viewer behaviors TikTok's recommendation system uses to evaluate and distribute videos. This is designing for measurable outcomes, not just creative expression.

## Core Principle

Design for behavior, not just expression. The algorithm doesn't evaluate whether your content is "good" — it measures what viewers do when they see it. Understanding which behaviors matter, and designing content that triggers them, is the difference between creating in a vacuum and creating for distribution.

## The Three Key Signals

### 1. Completion Rate
**What it is:** The percentage of viewers who watch the video to the end.
**Why it matters:** The single strongest signal that content is worth showing to more people.
**How to optimize:**
- Keep videos as short as the content allows (don't pad)
- Use retention structuring techniques (open loops, stepwise reveals)
- Place the payoff near the end so viewers have a reason to finish
- Remove any segment that causes drop-off

### 2. Rewatch Behavior
**What it is:** Viewers watching the video more than once.
**Why it matters:** Signals exceptionally high interest — the content was valuable enough to consume again.
**How to trigger:**
- Include dense information that can't be absorbed in one viewing
- Add visual details that reward a second look
- Create "wait, what?" moments that make viewers replay
- Use text overlays with more info than can be read in one pass
- Lists or tutorials that viewers need to reference again

### 3. Engagement Velocity
**What it is:** How quickly comments, shares, and saves accumulate after posting.
**Why it matters:** Fast engagement signals trending content worth pushing to broader audiences.
**How to accelerate:**
- Ask direct questions that viewers want to answer
- Make content shareable (useful to someone the viewer knows)
- Create save-worthy content (reference material, tutorials, lists)
- Post when your audience is active for faster initial engagement

## Designing for Each Signal

### Comments (Engagement Velocity)
- Ask for opinions: "Which one would you pick?"
- Invite debate: "Am I wrong about this?"
- Request experiences: "Has this happened to you?"
- Use mild controversy: "Unpopular opinion..."
- Leave something slightly incomplete so viewers comment to add

### Shares (Engagement Velocity + Distribution)
- Create content people want to send to a specific person
- "Tag someone who needs to see this" (but make it genuine)
- Useful tutorials get shared to "save for later" groups
- Relatable content gets shared as "this is literally us"

### Saves (Long-Term Signal)
- Tutorials and how-tos
- Lists and resources
- Reference content viewers will come back to
- Anything with "save this for later" energy

## The Behavioral Design Framework

For every video, before posting, ask:
1. **Will they finish?** (Is the structure tight enough for completion?)
2. **Will they rewatch?** (Is there a reason to see it again?)
3. **Will they engage?** (Is there a clear prompt or trigger for comments/shares/saves?)

If the answer to all three is yes, you've designed a video the algorithm can amplify.

## Skill Benchmark

Content is engineered to produce measurable behavioral signals, not just impressions.

## Common Mistakes

- Creating "good" content with no behavioral trigger (high quality, low distribution)
- Asking for engagement in a generic way ("like and follow!" is invisible now)
- Optimizing for one signal while ignoring others
- Ignoring analytics feedback about which signals your content actually generates

## Practice Drill

Review your last 10 videos' analytics. For each, identify which signal (completion, rewatch, engagement) was strongest and weakest. Design your next video specifically to improve the weakest signal.
