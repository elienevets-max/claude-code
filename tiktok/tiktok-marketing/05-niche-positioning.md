# Skill 5: Niche Positioning Clarity

## What It Is

Becoming recognizable for a specific value domain so that viewers, and the algorithm, know exactly what to expect from your account. This is your strategic identity on the platform.

## Why It Matters

Growth accelerates when there is zero ambiguity about what you offer. The algorithm categorizes creators to match them with interested viewers. If your content is scattered across topics, the algorithm can't find your audience. If viewers can't immediately understand your account's value, they won't follow.

## The Three Questions Every Viewer Asks

When someone lands on your profile, they subconsciously evaluate:

1. **What do you talk about?** — The topic or domain
2. **Who is it for?** — The target viewer
3. **Why are you worth following?** — The unique value or perspective

If all three answers are clear within seconds, follow rates increase dramatically.

## Execution Methods

### Consistent Topic Cluster
- Choose a core topic area and stay within it
- You can cover sub-topics, but they should all connect back to the core
- Examples:
  - Core: "Small business marketing" → Sub-topics: social media, email, branding, pricing psychology
  - Core: "Home cooking for beginners" → Sub-topics: knife skills, meal prep, grocery shopping, simple recipes
- Avoid random one-off videos on unrelated topics — they confuse the algorithm and dilute your audience

### Repeatable Format
- Develop 2-3 content formats you use consistently
- Formats create recognition: viewers see the visual structure and immediately know it's you
- Examples:
  - "Myth vs. Reality" series
  - "One tip a day" format
  - "Reacting to [niche topic]" series
  - Consistent visual template (same background, font, layout)
- Repeatable formats reduce production friction and increase output consistency

### Identifiable Perspective
- Your angle on the topic is what separates you from everyone else covering the same thing
- Perspective examples:
  - "Marketing advice from someone who's failed at it" (honesty angle)
  - "Cooking for people who hate cooking" (audience-specific angle)
  - "Design tips but make it chaotic" (personality angle)
- Your perspective is your brand — it should be evident in every video

## The One-Sentence Test

**If a viewer can describe your account in one sentence, your positioning is clear.**

Examples of passing the test:
- "That account that teaches marketing through memes"
- "The guy who explains money stuff for people in their 20s"
- "The chef who makes fancy food look easy"

Examples of failing the test:
- "They post... different stuff?"
- "Sometimes it's lifestyle, sometimes tips, sometimes vlogs"
- "I'm not sure what they're about exactly"

## Building Your Niche Position

### Step 1: Define Your Core
- What topic can you talk about endlessly?
- What do people already come to you for?
- Where does your knowledge/experience/passion intersect with audience demand?

### Step 2: Define Your Audience
- Be specific about who you're talking to
- "Everyone" is not a niche
- The more specific your audience, the stronger the connection

### Step 3: Define Your Angle
- What makes your take different from the 100 other accounts on this topic?
- Your lived experience, personality, format, or contrarian view

### Step 4: Commit and Iterate
- Stick with your positioning for at least 30-50 videos before pivoting
- Early results may be slow — the algorithm needs data to categorize you
- Refine within the niche rather than abandoning it

## Skill Benchmark

A new viewer can describe your account in one sentence after seeing 2-3 videos.

## Common Mistakes

- Trying to appeal to everyone (appeals to no one)
- Switching topics based on trends instead of staying in your lane
- Having a vague or generic positioning ("I help people succeed")
- Copying another creator's niche without adding a unique angle
- Confusing niche with narrow — you can cover broad themes through a specific lens

## Practice Drill

Write your account's one-sentence description. Show it to 5 people unfamiliar with your content. If they can predict what kind of videos you make from that sentence alone, your positioning is strong. If not, sharpen it.
