# Skill 7: Psychological Engagement Design

## What It Is

Prompting viewer interaction by leveraging natural human psychological tendencies. This is not about asking people to engage — it's about creating content structures that make engagement the instinctive response.

## Core Principle

Engagement is participation design, not audience reaction. The creator designs the interaction; the viewer simply responds to a well-constructed prompt. The best engagement triggers feel natural — the viewer comments because they *want to*, not because they were asked to.

## Effective Psychological Triggers

### Asking for Judgments
- Present a scenario and ask viewers to evaluate it
- "Was I right to do this?"
- "Rate this from 1-10"
- "Which option would you choose?"
- Why it works: Humans are compulsive evaluators — we can't see a question without forming an opinion, and once we have an opinion, we want to express it

### Inviting Correction
- Make a deliberate (or slightly imprecise) claim that knowledgeable viewers will want to correct
- "I think the best way to do X is..."
- State something 90% right — the 10% will drive comments
- Why it works: The desire to correct others is one of the strongest engagement drivers on the internet. People who correct you also boost your video's engagement metrics

### Polarizing Mild Opinions
- Take a stance that reasonable people can disagree on
- "Unpopular opinion: X is overrated"
- "I don't care what anyone says, Y is better than Z"
- Keep it mild enough to avoid toxicity but strong enough to split opinions
- Why it works: Disagreement creates discussion. Discussion creates engagement velocity. The key word is "mild" — you want debate, not hostility

### Relatable Scenarios
- Describe an experience your audience has had but hasn't articulated
- "That feeling when..."
- "Nobody talks about how..."
- "Tell me I'm not the only one who..."
- Why it works: Recognition is validating. When viewers feel seen, they comment to confirm, tag friends who relate, and save the content

## Advanced Engagement Structures

### The Incomplete List
- Share "5 of the best..." and leave room for viewers to add their own
- Viewers feel compelled to contribute what you "missed"
- Creates a collaborative dynamic that drives comments

### The False Binary
- Present two options and ask which is better
- "A or B?" forces a choice, and people love defending their choice
- Works especially well in visual format (split screen, comparison)

### The Challenge
- Dare viewers to try something or prove you wrong
- "I bet you can't..." or "Try this and tell me what happens"
- Creates a feedback loop: viewer tries it → comes back to comment

### The Prediction
- Make a prediction about the viewer
- "I can tell what kind of person you are by..."
- "If you made it this far, you're probably someone who..."
- Viewers engage to confirm or deny the prediction

## Designing Engagement Into Content

Instead of adding a "comment below!" CTA at the end, embed engagement triggers throughout:

1. **Opening:** Hook with a judgment question ("Would you do this?")
2. **Middle:** Include a mildly polarizing claim
3. **End:** Close with a relatable scenario that invites "same!" responses

The trigger should feel like a natural part of the content, not an appendage.

## Skill Benchmark

Viewers interact because the content structure makes interaction the natural response — not because they were explicitly asked.

## Common Mistakes

- Saying "comment below!" without giving a reason to comment (generic CTAs are invisible)
- Being too controversial (drives negative engagement and damages brand)
- Not leaving room for viewer contribution (if you say everything, there's nothing to add)
- Making engagement feel transactional ("I'll follow you back if you comment")
- Overusing the same trigger (audiences adapt — rotate your techniques)

## Practice Drill

For your next 5 videos, design a specific psychological engagement trigger before you record. After posting, track which trigger style generates the most comments. Build your engagement trigger library based on real data.
