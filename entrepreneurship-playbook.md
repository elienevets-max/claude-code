# The Entrepreneurship Playbook

> "I am good at only one thing — business. For the last 30 years I built 19 companies and invested in 78 startups." — Simon Squibb

A complete framework for starting, running, and growing a business from someone who has done it 19 times. Extracted from Simon Squibb's masterclass.

---

## How to Start a Business with No Money

### Step 0: It Starts with a Feeling, Not an Idea

A business doesn't start with an idea. It starts with a feeling — an instinct that you need to make a change. Then you apply yourself to learning what's the best way to build something you love.

### The Passion-First Principle

> "The school system has taught you the wrong thing — that if you're not good at something you need to spend more time getting better at it. That is a lie."

1. **Write down what you love doing** — get obsessed with it, get really good at it
2. **Write down what you hate doing** — outsource it, don't do it
3. **Apply a business mindset to what you love** — this is where the idea comes from

You don't need an original idea. Simon's company Fluid had 500 competitors doing almost the same thing. He won because he loved it more and pushed boundaries others wouldn't.

### The Three Steps to Launch

**Step 1: First Execution** — What is the simplest first thing you can do to bring your idea to life?
- A podcast, a blog post, a LinkedIn post, a social media handle
- Don't make it too hard — start with a rubbish microphone if you have to
- Execution will improve over time; just start

**Step 2: Experiment with Revenue** — Don't lock yourself into one revenue model from day one
- A photographer charging by the hour is limiting themselves
- Instead: post pictures online, test licensing, see what sticks
- Simon's agency charged by outcome (% of client sales), not by the hour — completely different from competitors
- Stay nimble like a boxer — see what punch combination works

**Step 3: Establish Purpose** — Your business needs a reason beyond profit
- If it's just for profit, everyone who works for you will want more money, and everyone who works with you will want to pay you less
- A purpose bigger than yourself means you won't be managing people — you'll be managing purpose
- This is one of the biggest stress-reducers in building a company

**The Airbnb Example:** Before they built the platform, they sold cereal boxes at political conventions. That's where they met their first customers, made their first revenue, and funded the early business. Start where you can, not where you want to end up.

## How to Win

### 1. Delayed Gratification

> "Delayed gratification is the number one reason that businesses win."

- Don't rush to charge customers — let them help you succeed first
- Simon did his first client's work for free. That client stayed for 16 years and recommended him to everyone
- Facebook spent nearly 10 years before monetizing. YouTube started with no revenue. Google started with no revenue.
- Build value first, monetize later

**The Warning:** When Facebook started monetizing aggressively, it declined as a brand — too many ads, annoying features, prioritizing advertisers over users.

### 2. Culture Eats Strategy

Build a customer-centric culture:
- Don't think about how to extract money from your customer
- Think about how to bring value
- If you want loyalty, sharing, word-of-mouth — show them love
- People don't do this because they're too busy not waiting for the payoff

### 3. Hack Your Luck

Luck is not subjective. Luck is hackable through three methods:

| Method | How It Works |
|--------|-------------|
| **Be Persistent** | Outlast your competition. Simon's competitors all shut down, went bankrupt. He cared more. |
| **Know Your Destination** | Define what success means for you personally — not what others say it should be |
| **Take Risk** | "The harder you work the luckier you get" is a lie. What equals success is taking risk. Learn to lean into fear, embrace it, and take more risk. |

> "If it was true that working hard equals success, every nurse in this country would be a millionaire."

## How to Lose

> "80% of A students end up working for D students because A students are scared to lose."

### Why Learning to Lose Is Essential

The education system taught you that success = getting an A. Wrong. Success = accepting failure, bouncing back, learning from it.

**Simon's proof:** If he hadn't lost a million pounds on a comic book business, he wouldn't be rich or successful today. He needed to learn how to fail.

### Three Rules of Losing

1. **Don't let things own you** — If you let possessions own you, you're controlled by the very thing meant to bring you pleasure

2. **Let your short-term ego go** — Don't drive the right car to impress people who don't care about you. Let people underestimate you — it is incredibly powerful to be underestimated

3. **Power up your internal ego** — Replace the external ego (status, image) with an internal ego (knowing where you're going regardless of what others think)

> "If you think that the worst is if you try something and you lose everything — it doesn't matter. You can go again."

## Mind Maps vs. Business Plans

> "I have never seen anyone teach anybody how to do a mind map. There's no template to sell you — that's why no one teaches it."

### Why Business Plans Kill Companies

- Business plans are too linear — a 20-page document locks you in
- People stick to the plan and die
- Mind maps leave you nimble and free to explore

### How to Build a Mind Map

1. **Center:** Your hobby / what you love doing
2. **First ring:** The business — how does it connect to what you love?
3. **Branches:** Every direction the business could go

**Simon's actual mind map for his platform:**

```
                    Network ← Podcast
                                  ↑
Brands ← GoDaddy, Tide, Adobe    |
                                  |
           HOBBY/PASSION ← BUSINESS (Free Humanity)
                                  |
                                  ↓
Team → Editor, Accountant    App → Free Help → Connected to Network
  ↓
Partners (from podcast)      Merchandising → T-shirts, Sweets (Buss's)
```

### Key Advantages of Mind Maps

- **Infinite** — just like your brain, a mind map never stops growing
- **Connectable** — you can link dots between different branches (the podcast network feeds into the app's free help content)
- **Evolving** — add bubbles as things change, people change, the world changes
- **Future-ready** — when Simon saw "sweets" on his mind map, he started noting anyone with confectionery experience during interviews. Four years later, he launched the brand.
- **Free** — no template to buy, no product to sell you

## How to Find Purpose

### The Wrong Question vs. The Right Question

| The School System Asks | You Should Ask |
|----------------------|---------------|
| "What are you going to do when you grow up?" | "What problem are you going to solve?" |

### Three Steps to Finding Purpose

**Step 1: Think about it.** This sounds obvious, but people don't do it. They're afraid of being laughed at, or they overthink it and give up.

**Step 2: What problem matters to you?** Start small — even "the bank isn't open early enough" or "the swimming pool doesn't let me swim when I want to." Your brain wakes up the entrepreneurial muscle when you start thinking about problems.

**Step 3: Match your life to that purpose.** This is often easier than you think. The gap between what people are doing and what their dream is often just a 3% difference — like the 3% DNA difference between chimpanzees and humans.

> "I've met hundreds and hundreds of people who tell me what their dream is and it turns out that their dream is often just 3% different from their existing life."

### You Don't Have to Do It Alone

- Find others working on the same problem
- Join their organization — but ask for equity
- 1 + 1 = 11 when you combine forces with aligned people

> "It's harder to work for someone else than work for yourself — despite what you have been told."

## How to Find a Co-Founder

### Why Get a Co-Founder?

Like a gym buddy — accountability. You go more often when someone is counting on you.

> "I would rather have 50% of a business I enjoy that's successful than 100% of a nightmare and a failure."

### The Three-Step Process

**Step 1: Define the Opposite**

Write down in two columns:
- **What you LOVE doing** — this is your domain
- **What you HATE doing** — this is what your co-founder should love

You want someone with the opposite skill set who won't cross over into your territory.

**Step 2: Check the Moral Code**

This is like finding a life partner. Use open questions and philosophy to test alignment.

**The Madoff Test:** Ask them: "What if for the next 40 years you'll have the most amazing life — three houses, private jets, everyone loves you. But at 70, everyone discovers you're a financial fraud. Do you take the deal?"

About 50% of people say yes. That tells you everything about their values.

**Step 3: Post It**

Tell everyone you're looking. Post on LinkedIn. Ask people. Look for this person everywhere — cafes, dinner, the supermarket. The Red Car Theory: once you define what you're looking for, you start seeing it everywhere.

> Write down every detail about your ideal co-founder — how they speak, their background, their strengths. The more detailed you are, the more likely you are to manifest it.

## How to Sell

### Principle 1: Sell the Sizzle, Not the Steak

Steve Jobs never said "here's a phone with an Intel processor." He talked about how the product was for game changers, for creative types, for people who think differently.

Your accountant can be your best salesperson — if you give her the sizzle. Simon's accountant would tell her CFO friends: "We saved a company from going bankrupt. We made one company an extra million pounds a month." That's the sizzle.

### Principle 2: The Three-Step Sales Process

Most people start at Step 3. You need Steps 1 and 2 first.

| Step | Focus | Key Question |
|------|-------|-------------|
| **1. Research** | Do they need you? | Have you understood their business deeply enough to know if you can help? |
| **2. Relationship** | Do you like each other? | Is the connection genuine? If they're rude to the waiter, walk away. |
| **3. The Deal** | Structure the agreement | If Steps 1 and 2 are done right, this happens naturally. |

> "If they need you and they like you, they will work with you to make a deal happen."

### Principle 3: The Long Game

**Harvard study on salespeople:**
- Top 50 salespeople: approach someone **5 times** max, then consider the lead dead
- Top 1% of salespeople: persistent indefinitely with a system

Simon wrote down 50 target companies on day one. Every single month for years, he contacted each one — Christmas cards, Chinese New Year greetings, useful industry research. Some clients took **9 years** of monthly contact to land.

**Build systems for persistence:**
- Email lists (still powerful, despite not being "cool")
- Monthly touchpoints that aren't always a pitch
- Genuine relationship-building over years

### Principle 4: Be Yourself

> "The best salespeople tell me 'you don't want this car — you want that car in another showroom.' I hired that person."

Bring your personality. Be honest. Be authentic. Think long-term, not commission.

## How to Market Your Business

### Rule 1: Know Your Customer

> "50% of what you spend on marketing will probably be wasted."

Start by understanding exactly who your customer is. Facebook started in universities — they knew their audience. They built a "single or available" feature that became what people talked about. The product created the marketing.

**The Holy Grail of marketing:** Build something into your product that people talk about naturally.

### Rule 2: The Staircase Philosophy

Do something wild and unexpected that symbolizes your brand, then evolve it:

1. **The Hook:** Simon bought a staircase at auction for £26,000 when it made the news
2. **The PR Wave:** Every news channel that reported the staircase sale wanted to know why — millions in free coverage
3. **The Evolution:** Added a doorbell where people could pitch their dreams — gave the press a follow-up story
4. **The Partnership:** Ring/Amazon saw it, became a partner — they pay for the marketing

> "What's your staircase? What's going to make you stand out?"

### Rule 3: Build Systems

Whatever marketing channel you choose, systematize it:
- One core video, edited in-app for each platform
- Tailored to each platform's nuances (fonts, image formats)
- Better to do one platform really well than all of them badly
- But with the right system, you can do all of them with the same resources as one

### Rule 4: Marketing Should Be Fun

> "If you don't enjoy it, what's the point?"

Apply marketing to what you naturally enjoy:
- Love writing? → Email marketing, LinkedIn
- Love talking? → Podcasts, video
- Don't force yourself onto TikTok if you hate camera work

**Starbucks' Early Marketing:**
1. Opened stores close together instead of buying billboards — each store was a marketing touchpoint
2. Treated baristas as partners — gave them full medical care even if part-time. Happy staff = your best marketing

## How to PR Your Business

### Hack 1: Target Precisely

Getting in the BBC feels good but may not drive sales. PR should be strategic — reach the audience that will actually become customers.

If you're selling cherries in East Sussex, the East Sussex Times is more valuable than the BBC.

### Hack 2: Do the Journalist's Job for Them

> "Journalists are lazy. If you provide everything for them, they don't need to do any work."

- Write the press release as if it's the actual published story
- Write headlines that serve the reader, not you
- Include high-resolution photos
- Research the journalist — write in their style
- Make it so all they have to do is say yes and add their own touch

### Hack 3: Build Direct Relationships

- Follow journalists on Twitter/X — many have small followings and few comments
- Comment on their stories, engage with their posts
- Become part of their thinking: "I need a quote for this story — who do I know?"
- Don't outsource to PR agencies early on — direct relationships are more powerful and free

### Hack 4: You Are the Brand's PR Engine

Whatever you post on social media reflects on your business. Brands check your social media before working with you. Journalists won't risk their job writing about someone who's out of control online. Be disciplined.
