## Master Decision Tree: Which Framework Do You Need?

**Start here → What's the core problem?**

### "I don't know where my next customers are coming from"

→ **Traction: Bullseye Method** — Score all 19 channels, pick top 3, run cheap tests, double down on the winner

→ Then: **DotCom Secrets: Traffic Temperature** — Classify your traffic as cold/warm/hot and match each to the right funnel entry point

→ Then: **DotCom Secrets: Three Traffic Types** — Convert traffic you control and don't control into traffic you own (email list)

### "I have traffic but it's not converting"

→ First check: **Are you sending traffic to a website or a funnel?** (DotCom Secrets) — If it's a website with navigation, that's the problem. Build a funnel.

→ Then: **StoryBrand BrandScript** — Is your messaging clear? Does the customer understand in 5 seconds what you offer, how it makes their life better, and what to do next?

→ Then: **DotCom Secrets: Funnel Architecture** — Select the right funnel for your price point (Lead Funnel, Unboxing Funnel, Webinar Funnel, or Application Funnel)

→ Then: **Positioning** — Are you competing in a crowded category or have you carved out a category you own?

### "My customer acquisition cost is too high / ads aren't profitable"

→ **DotCom Secrets: Funnel Math** — What's your average cart value? Have you added order bumps, upsells, and downsells?

→ **DotCom Secrets: Value Ladder** — Do you have a backend? Front-end should acquire customers (even at a loss), backend creates profit

→ **Ecommerce Evolved: Customer Value Journey** — Are you moving customers through all 8 stages (Awareness → Promotion) or just focused on the transaction?

→ Key principle: "Whoever can spend the most to acquire a customer wins" — fix the backend before trying to fix the ads

### "I have a product but no system to sell it"

→ **DotCom Secrets: Value Ladder** — Map your product suite from low-ticket entry to high-ticket premium

→ **DotCom Secrets: Funnel Selection** — Match each rung to the right funnel type

→ **StoryBrand: BrandScript** — Script your messaging using the 7-part framework

→ **DotCom Secrets: Hook, Story, Offer** — Apply this to every ad, email, and content piece

→ **DotCom Secrets: Email Sequences** — Build Soap Opera Sequence (days 1-5) then Seinfeld Sequence (ongoing)

### "My messaging isn't landing / I don't know how to talk about my product"

→ **StoryBrand: 7-Part BrandScript** — Customer is the hero, you are the guide. Define: Character, Problem (external, internal, philosophical), Guide, Plan, Call to Action, Failure to Avoid, Success

→ **Positioning: Own One Word** — What single word or concept do you own in the customer's mind?

→ **DotCom Secrets: Attractive Character** — Build your brand personality with backstory, parables, flaws, and polarity

→ **DotCom Secrets: Epiphany Bridge** — Sell through story, not logic. Take them through YOUR moment of realization.

### "I need to sell to businesses (B2B), not just consumers"

→ **Ultimate Sales Machine: Dream 100** — Identify the 100 accounts that would transform your business. Market to them relentlessly.

→ **Predictable Revenue: Separate Prospecting from Closing** — Even if one person does both, the process should be distinct

→ **Predictable Revenue: Three Lead Types** — Seeds (referrals, best quality), Nets (marketing, medium quality), Spears (outbound, most controllable)

→ **Ultimate Sales Machine: Education-Based Selling** — Stop pitching. Teach. Position yourself as the expert who educates before selling.

→ For high-ticket B2B: **DotCom Secrets: Application Funnel** — Flip the dynamic so you're selecting clients, not chasing them

### "I'm doing everything myself and can't scale"

→ **E-Myth: Work ON the business, not IN it** — Systematize every repeatable task as if you're building a franchise

→ **Traction EOS: Six Key Components** — Vision, People, Data, Issues, Process, Traction. Score yourself on each.

→ **Clockwork: Design the business to run without you** — Identify your Queen Bee Role (the one activity that drives everything), protect it, delegate the rest

→ **Traction EOS: Rocks** — Set 3-7 ninety-day priorities. Everything else is noise.

→ **Ultimate Sales Machine: Time Management Mastery** — Touch it once. If it takes less than 5 minutes, do it now. Batch everything else.

### "I need to sell something at $2,000+"

→ **DotCom Secrets: Application Funnel** — Lead page → Application → Phone/video call → Close

→ **DotCom Secrets: Change the Selling Environment** — Sales pages can't close high-ticket alone. You need human interaction.

→ **DotCom Secrets: Perfect Webinar Script** — Can work for $297-$2,000. Break three false beliefs, then stack the offer.

→ **DotCom Secrets: Value Ladder** — Make sure you've built trust through lower rungs before asking for the big commitment

## How the Frameworks Connect (System Map)

**Layer 1: FIND CUSTOMERS**

Traction (Bullseye Method) → identifies your best growth channels

DotCom Secrets (Traffic Types) → classifies traffic and converts it all to email

**Layer 2: COMMUNICATE CLEARLY**

Positioning (Own One Word) → defines your strategic position in the market

StoryBrand (BrandScript) → scripts your messaging on every page, ad, and email

DotCom Secrets (Attractive Character) → gives your brand a human personality

**Layer 3: CONVERT & MONETIZE**

DotCom Secrets (Value Ladder) → structures your offer suite from free to premium

DotCom Secrets (Funnel Architecture) → builds the conversion system for each offer

Ecommerce Evolved (Customer Value Journey) → maps the full 8-stage lifecycle

DotCom Secrets (Epiphany Bridge + Perfect Webinar) → the actual selling scripts

**Layer 4: SYSTEMATIZE SALES**

Ultimate Sales Machine (Dream 100) → targets your highest-value accounts

Predictable Revenue (Pipeline Model) → makes revenue repeatable and forecastable

Ultimate Sales Machine (Education-Based Selling) → positions you as the expert

**Layer 5: SCALE OPERATIONS**

E-Myth (Systematize Everything) → document and delegate repeatable processes

Traction EOS (Rocks + Scorecard) → quarterly priorities and weekly metrics

Clockwork (Queen Bee Role) → identify and protect the one thing that drives everything

**The flow:** Find channels (Layer 1) → Craft clear messaging (Layer 2) → Build funnels that convert (Layer 3) → Systematize sales so they're repeatable (Layer 4) → Build operations so you can scale without burning out (Layer 5)

## Quick Reference by Book

### DotCom Secrets (Brunson)

**Use for:** Funnel architecture, offer design, upsell sequences, webinar scripts, email follow-up, traffic strategy, value ladder design

**Core principle:** Whoever can spend the most to acquire a customer wins. Engineer your funnel math to make that possible.

### Traction (Weinberg & Mares)

**Use for:** Choosing growth channels, testing marketing strategies, finding product-market fit for marketing

**Core principle:** Systematically test all 19 channels. The best channel is rarely the one you'd guess. Run cheap tests, measure, double down.

### Building a StoryBrand (Miller)

**Use for:** Website copy, ad messaging, brand positioning, homepage rewrites, email subject lines, any communication that needs clarity

**Core principle:** Customer is the hero. You are the guide. If your messaging confuses, you lose. Clarity wins.

### The Ultimate Sales Machine (Holmes)

**Use for:** B2B sales processes, Dream 100 outreach, time management, education-based selling, training sales teams

**Core principle:** Mastery isn't about doing 4,000 things. It's about doing 12 things 4,000 times each with pigheaded discipline.

### Ecommerce Evolved (Larsson)

**Use for:** E-commerce customer journeys, retention strategy, moving customers from first purchase to advocacy

**Core principle:** Stop optimizing the transaction. Design the entire 8-stage journey from awareness to promotion.

### Predictable Revenue (Ross)

**Use for:** B2B pipeline building, separating prospecting from closing, lead generation systems

**Core principle:** Three lead types (Seeds, Nets, Spears) need three different processes. Separate the functions even if one person does them all.

### Positioning (Ries & Trout)

**Use for:** Competitive strategy, category creation, brand positioning, differentiating from competitors

**Core principle:** Own one word in the customer's mind. If you can't be first in an existing category, create a new category you can be first in.

### The E-Myth Revisited (Gerber)

**Use for:** Systematizing operations, delegating tasks, building processes, freeing the founder

**Core principle:** Work ON the business, not IN it. Build every process as if you're creating a franchise manual.

### Traction EOS (Wickman)

**Use for:** Setting quarterly goals, running weekly meetings, tracking metrics, resolving organizational issues

**Core principle:** Six components — Vision, People, Data, Issues, Process, Traction. Set 90-day Rocks. Execute weekly.

### Clockwork (Michalowicz)

**Use for:** Designing a business that runs without you, identifying the most important work, delegation frameworks

**Core principle:** Identify your Queen Bee Role — the single activity that generates the most value. Protect it ruthlessly. Delegate everything else.

## Conflict Resolution: When Books Disagree

**StoryBrand says "the customer is the hero" vs. DotCom Secrets says "build an Attractive Character"**

These don't actually conflict. StoryBrand's framework is for your MESSAGING — how you talk about the customer on your website, ads, and emails. DotCom Secrets' Attractive Character is for your BRAND PERSONALITY — the voice and persona behind all communication. The customer is the hero of the story, but the guide (you, the Attractive Character) is the one telling it. Use both: StoryBrand structures WHAT you say, Attractive Character determines HOW you say it.

**Traction says "test many channels" vs. Ultimate Sales Machine says "pigheaded discipline on a few things"**

Sequential, not contradictory. Use Traction's Bullseye to FIND the right channels through rapid testing. Then apply Holmes' discipline to EXECUTE on the winners. Testing phase is 2-4 weeks. Execution phase is ongoing.

**DotCom Secrets says "free + shipping" vs. Positioning says "premium positioning"**

Depends on your value ladder position. A free + shipping offer IS premium if the product itself is high-quality and the experience is excellent. Brunson's free book still positioned him as an authority. The free offer is a customer acquisition mechanism, not a devaluation of the brand. What matters is what comes after — the upsell sequence and backend must match the premium positioning.

## StoryBrand BrandScript Template (from Building a StoryBrand by Miller)

| Element | Question to Answer |
|---------|-------------------|
| 1. A Character (Customer) | Who is your customer and what do they want? |
| 2. Has a Problem | External problem (tangible), Internal problem (how it makes them feel), Philosophical problem (why it's just wrong) |
| 3. And Meets a Guide | Your brand — demonstrate empathy ("we understand") and authority ("here's why we can help") |
| 4. Who Gives Them a Plan | 3 simple steps that reduce risk and confusion |
| 5. And Calls Them to Action | Direct CTA (buy now) + Transitional CTA (free guide, webinar) |
| 6. That Helps Them Avoid Failure | What bad things happen if they DON'T act? |
| 7. And Ends in Success | What does their life look like after? Paint the picture. |

Every page, ad, and email should map to this framework.

## The Dream 100 Process (from The Ultimate Sales Machine by Holmes)

1. Identify the 100 companies, individuals, or accounts that would transform your business if they became customers
2. Organize into categories (by industry, geography, size, etc.)
3. Research each: decision-maker name, email, phone, social media
4. Begin a 12-touch outreach sequence over 12 weeks (email, call, package, social)
5. Personalize every touch — generic outreach gets ignored
6. Use education-based selling: teach them something valuable before asking for anything
7. Be relentless — most people quit after 3-4 touches. The sale often happens at touch 8-12.
8. Track everything in a CRM or spreadsheet

## Customer Value Journey — 8 Stages (from Ecommerce Evolved by Larsson)

| Stage | Goal | Key Tactic |
|-------|------|------------|
| 1. Awareness | Stranger discovers you exist | Content, ads, SEO, social |
| 2. Engagement | Stranger interacts with your content | Blog posts, social engagement, videos |
| 3. Subscribe | Visitor gives email | Lead magnet, free guide, checklist |
| 4. Convert | First purchase | Low-ticket entry offer, free + shipping |
| 5. Excite | Buyer has amazing first experience | Premium packaging, fast delivery, follow-up |
| 6. Ascend | Buyer upgrades or buys more | Upsells, bundles, VIP programs, higher-ticket offers |
| 7. Advocate | Customer tells others | Referral program, testimonial requests |
| 8. Promote | Customer actively markets for you | Affiliate program, user-generated content campaigns |

Most businesses only focus on stages 3-4. The full journey from 1-8 is where exponential growth happens.

## Predictable Revenue: Three Lead Types (from Predictable Revenue by Ross)

| Lead Type | Source | Quality | Control |
|-----------|--------|---------|---------|
| Seeds | Referrals, word-of-mouth | Highest | Lowest (organic) |
| Nets | Marketing campaigns, content, ads | Medium | Medium |
| Spears | Outbound prospecting, cold outreach | Variable | Highest (you choose who to target) |

Each type needs a different process. Even if one person handles all three, the mindset and workflow should be distinct. Separate prospecting activity from closing activity.

## Operations Frameworks

### E-Myth: The Franchise Prototype

Every repeatable task should be documented as if you're writing a franchise manual. Ask: "Could someone else do this with just the documentation?" If not, the process isn't systemized enough.

### Traction EOS: The Six Key Components

1. **Vision** — Where are you going? Does everyone know?
2. **People** — Right people in right seats?
3. **Data** — Are you running on facts or feelings? Weekly scorecard.
4. **Issues** — Do you identify, discuss, and solve issues weekly?
5. **Process** — Are your core processes documented and followed?
6. **Traction** — 90-day Rocks (3-7 priorities). Weekly meetings. Accountability.

### Clockwork: The Queen Bee Role

Identify the single activity in your business that generates the most value. Everything else exists to support and protect that activity. Delegate everything that isn't the QBR. Design your calendar and team around maximizing time spent on the QBR.

## Universal Diagnostic: 10 Questions to Ask Any Business

1. **Value Ladder:** How many rungs do you have? (If one, that's a problem) — *DotCom Secrets*
2. **Channels:** Which of the 19 traction channels have you actually tested? — *Traction*
3. **Messaging clarity:** Can a stranger understand in 5 seconds what you offer, how it helps, and what to do? — *StoryBrand*
4. **Positioning:** What word do you own in the customer's mind? — *Positioning*
5. **Funnel math:** What's your cost per lead, cost per buyer, and average cart value? — *DotCom Secrets*
6. **Dream 100:** Who are the 100 accounts that would transform your business, and are you marketing to them? — *Ultimate Sales Machine*
7. **Customer journey:** Are you designing all 8 stages or just the transaction? — *Ecommerce Evolved*
8. **Email list:** What percentage of your traffic are you converting to traffic you own? — *DotCom Secrets*
9. **Systems:** Could someone else run your business for 4 weeks without you? — *E-Myth + Clockwork*
10. **90-Day Rocks:** What are your 3-7 priorities this quarter, and are you executing weekly? — *Traction EOS*
