# The Perfect Sparkler Exit

### A Guide for Wedding Planners and Photographers

---

## Why Sparkler Exits Matter

The sparkler send-off is consistently one of the most photographed, most shared, and most emotionally resonant moments of a wedding. It's the image that ends up on Instagram, framed on the mantle, and remembered by guests for years.

For planners, offering a well-executed sparkler exit is a differentiator — it elevates the entire event and produces stunning portfolio content.

This guide covers everything you need to plan a flawless sparkler exit from start to finish.

---

## Before the Wedding: Planning Checklist

### 1. Choose the Right Sparkler Size

| Size | Burn Time | Best For |
|------|-----------|----------|
| 10" | ~45 seconds | Intimate ceremonies under 50 guests |
| 20" | 2 - 2.5 minutes | Standard exits, indoor venues, most weddings |
| 36" | 3 - 4 minutes | Grand exits, large venues, professional photography |

**Recommendation:** For most weddings, the **20-inch** sparkler is the go-to. If the couple wants a premium, dramatic exit with extra time for photography, go with the **36-inch**.

### 2. Calculate Quantity

**Formula:** 1 sparkler per guest + 10% buffer

| Guests | Sparklers Needed |
|:------:|:----------------:|
| 75 | 83 |
| 100 | 110 |
| 150 | 165 |
| 200 | 220 |

Always order the buffer. A few extra sparklers cost almost nothing, but running short means some guests are standing in the dark during the send-off.

### 3. Coordinate with the Venue

Before the wedding, confirm:
- [ ] Venue allows sparklers (check their policy in writing)
- [ ] Indoor or outdoor exit? (Smokeless sparklers are recommended for indoor)
- [ ] Adequate ventilation if indoors (6+ air changes per hour)
- [ ] Ceiling height is 12+ feet for indoor use
- [ ] Fire extinguisher within 50 feet of exit path
- [ ] Designated sparkler disposal area (sand bucket or water bucket)

### 4. Brief the Photographer

Share this timing guide with the photographer at least one week before the wedding. The photographer's positioning and camera settings are critical — a last-minute sparkler exit without preparation leads to blown-out or blurry images.

## Setting Up the Send-Off

### Formation: Two Lines, Not a Cluster

The #1 mistake is letting guests bunch up in a crowd. Instead:
- Arrange guests in **two parallel lines** facing each other
- Lines should be **5-6 feet apart** (enough room for the couple to walk between comfortably)
- Each guest stands at **arm's length** from the person next to them
- The lines should start at the venue exit and extend toward the couple's departure vehicle or end point

### Lighting Strategy: Waves, Not All at Once

If everyone lights their sparkler simultaneously, half will burn out before the couple appears.

**The wave method:**
1. Assign **2-3 "sparkler wranglers"** (bridal party members or coordinators) with long-reach lighters
2. Wranglers position themselves at the **far end** of the line (closest to the exit door)
3. Light the **back of the line first**, working forward
4. By the time the front of the line is lit, the couple walks out — sparklers are burning at full brightness across the entire tunnel

### Designated Lighter Team

- Use **long-reach utility lighters** (not matches or pocket lighters)
- Each wrangler can light approximately 20-25 sparklers in 60 seconds
- For 100+ guests, use 3 wranglers minimum
- Lighters should be tested beforehand — cold or wind can cause issues with cheap lighters

## Timing the Walk

This is the most important detail. The couple must walk at the right moment for peak brightness.

| Sparkler Size | When to Signal the Couple | Couple's Walk Speed |
|:-------------:|:-------------------------:|:-------------------:|
| 10" | **Immediately** after final sparkler is lit | Brisk walk |
| 20" | **15 seconds** after final sparkler is lit | Natural, relaxed pace |
| 36" | **30 seconds** after final sparkler is lit | Slow, enjoy the moment |

**Golden rule:** The couple should walk **slowly**. Not a sprint, not a jog — a slow, deliberate stroll. This gives the photographer time for multiple shots, guests time to enjoy the moment, and the couple time to actually experience it.

Tell the couple in advance: "Walk like you're on a red carpet, not running to catch a flight."

## Photography Tips

### Camera Settings for Sparkler Exits
- **Shutter speed:** 1/200s to 1/400s (fast enough to freeze sparkler motion without blur)
- **Aperture:** f/2.0 to f/2.8 (wide open to capture light)
- **ISO:** 1600-3200 (sparklers are bright, but the surrounding area is dark)
- **Flash:** Optional rear-curtain sync for creative effect. Many photographers skip flash entirely and rely on sparkler light.
- **White balance:** Tungsten or 3200K (sparklers burn warm gold — tungsten balance keeps colors natural without an orange cast)

### Photographer Positioning
- **Primary shooter:** At the **end** of the sparkler tunnel, shooting back through the lines toward the couple. This creates the iconic "tunnel of light" shot.
- **Second shooter (if available):** At a **45-degree angle** from the side, capturing the couple's profiles against the sparkler glow. This is the "Instagram angle" — the shot that looks best cropped to portrait orientation.
- **Avoid:** Standing behind the couple and shooting forward into the sparklers. This backlit angle is the hardest to expose correctly and rarely produces the best results.

### Video Considerations
- **Slow motion (60fps or 120fps):** Sparkler exits look spectacular in slow motion. Alert the videographer so they switch to a high frame rate before the send-off.
- **Audio:** The crackling sound of 100+ sparklers is atmospheric. Remind the videographer to capture ambient audio, not just the couple's mic.

## Safety Checklist

### Before the Send-Off
- [ ] Place a **sand bucket or water bucket** at each end of the sparkler line for disposal
- [ ] Confirm no loose sleeves, flowing veils, or trailing fabrics will be near lit sparklers
- [ ] Brief guests: "Hold sparklers at arm's length, away from hair and clothing"
- [ ] Children under 12 should hold sparklers only with a parent's hand guiding them — or skip sparklers for young kids entirely
- [ ] Assign a **sparkler wrangler** to collect spent sparklers immediately after the send-off

### During the Send-Off
- [ ] Wranglers light sparklers in waves (back to front)
- [ ] No one waves sparklers near another person's face or body
- [ ] Spent sparklers go directly into the disposal bucket — **not on the ground**
- [ ] Wranglers monitor for any dropped or mishandled sparklers

### After the Send-Off
- [ ] Collect all spent sparklers from disposal buckets
- [ ] Do a sweep of the area for any sparklers that were dropped
- [ ] Allow spent sparklers to cool for 10 minutes before bagging for disposal
- [ ] Verify no scorch marks or debris need to be addressed before the venue closes

## Common Mistakes That Ruin the Sparkler Exit

### 1. Buying Cheap, Short-Burning Sparklers
Generic sparklers from discount stores often burn for 20-30 seconds instead of the listed time, produce excessive smoke, and drop hot ash. The cost difference between budget and premium sparklers is $50-$100 total for a typical wedding. On a $30,000+ wedding budget, this is not the place to cut corners.

### 2. Lighting All Sparklers at Once
Half the sparklers burn out before the couple walks through. Use the wave lighting method described above.

### 3. Cluster Formation Instead of Two Lines
Guests naturally want to crowd around the couple. Without direction, they'll form a blob. The DJ or coordinator must **announce the formation** clearly: "Please form two lines from the door to the parking lot."

### 4. No Photography Coordination
The photographer didn't know the sparkler exit was happening, wasn't in position, or didn't adjust settings. Communicate the plan at least one week in advance.

### 5. No Safety Plan
No disposal buckets, no wrangler, no guest briefing. One dropped sparkler on a tulle skirt can turn a fairy-tale ending into an incident report.

### 6. Wrong Timing for the Walk
The couple sprints through before the photographer can shoot, or waits too long and sparklers burn out. Use the timing chart above and rehearse with the couple if possible.
