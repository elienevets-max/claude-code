# Wedding Season Timing Guide

### When Planners Book, When to Reach Out, and When to Follow Up

---

## The Wedding Industry Calendar

Understanding the wedding booking cycle is critical for B2B outreach. Planners don't buy sparklers in June for a June wedding — they lock in vendors months in advance.

---

## Month-by-Month Breakdown

### January
- **Industry activity:** Engagement season peaks (holiday proposals). Planners onboarding new clients for spring/summer weddings. Vendor selection underway.
- **Outreach action:** Begin prospecting. Research your Dream 25 targets. Prepare outreach materials and sample kits.

### February
- **Industry activity:** Peak booking season for June weddings (4-month lead time). Valentine's proposals drive second engagement wave. Wedding expos and bridal shows.
- **Outreach action:** Finalize your Dream 25 list. Warm up prospects on social media (follow, engage with their content).

### March
- **Industry activity:** Planners are locking in final vendors for May-June weddings. Late bookings for summer.
- **Outreach action:** **LAUNCH EMAIL SEQUENCE — March 1.** This is the critical window. Planners are actively adding vendors for the upcoming season.
- **Inventory action:** Stock up on 36" and 20" gold sparklers (March 15 deadline). Print and ship wedding lookbooks to top prospects.

### April
- **Industry activity:** Final vendor confirmations. Planners running through logistics for spring weddings.
- **Outreach action:** Follow up on all prospects from March outreach. Send sample kits to interested planners. Schedule discovery calls.
- **Marketing action:** Launch wedding-themed social media calendar (20 posts). Activate Google Ads on wedding sparkler keywords.

### May
- **Industry activity:** **WEDDING SEASON BEGINS.** Early-season weddings start. Planners are in execution mode.
- **Outreach action:** Shift from prospecting to servicing. Fast response times are critical — planners ordering last-minute need same-day shipping.

### June
- **Industry activity:** **PEAK WEDDING MONTH.** Highest volume of weddings. Planners are fully booked and busy.
- **Outreach action:** Minimal cold outreach — planners are too busy to respond. Focus on servicing existing accounts and being responsive. Gather testimonials and photos from events you've supplied.

### July - August
- **Industry activity:** Summer wedding season continues. Second peak for outdoor/destination weddings.
- **Outreach action:** Continue servicing. Begin collecting case studies and social proof from season. Some planners start booking fall weddings.

### September
- **Industry activity:** Late-season weddings. Fall wedding bookings accelerating. Planners starting to plan for next year.
- **Outreach action:** Re-engage prospects who didn't convert in spring. Fall is a strong secondary booking window.

### October
- **Industry activity:** Fall weddings. Planners evaluating vendors for next year. Annual contract renewal discussions.
- **Outreach action:** **POST-SEASON FOLLOW-UP.** Reach out to planners you supplied this year. Propose annual contracts. Share season performance data.

### November - December
- **Industry activity:** Holiday engagement season begins (Thanksgiving through New Year's). Planners are booking 2027 weddings. Vendor selection for next year underway.
- **Outreach action:** Lock in annual contracts for next year. Holiday touchpoint — send a thank-you or small gift to key accounts. Start building next year's Dream 25 list.

## The Three Outreach Phases

### Phase 1: Pre-Season Prospecting (January - April)

**Goal:** Build relationships and secure vendor status before wedding season starts.

| Week | Action | Priority |
|------|--------|----------|
| Jan Week 1-2 | Research and build Dream 25 list | High |
| Jan Week 3-4 | Prepare email templates, sample kits, and collateral | High |
| Feb Week 1-2 | Engage prospects on Instagram (follow, like, comment) | Medium |
| Feb Week 3-4 | Attend or research local bridal expos for leads | Medium |
| **Mar Week 1** | **Send Email 1 (Value Lead) to all 25 targets** | **Critical** |
| Mar Week 1 | Send Email 2 (Social Proof) — 3 days after Email 1 | High |
| Mar Week 2 | Send Email 3 (Resource Drop) — 5 days after Email 2 | High |
| Mar Week 3 | Send Email 4 (Partnership Pitch) — 7 days after Email 3 | High |
| Mar Week 4 | Send Email 5 (Graceful Exit) — 7 days after Email 4 | High |
| Apr Week 1-2 | Follow up on all engaged prospects, schedule calls | High |
| Apr Week 3-4 | Ship sample kits, close early deals | High |

### Phase 2: Active Season Servicing (May - September)

**Goal:** Deliver exceptional service to converted accounts. Gather social proof.

- Respond to all planner inquiries within 1 hour (B2B priority SLA)
- Ensure same-day shipping on all orders placed before 3 PM
- Ask satisfied planners for Instagram tags and testimonials
- Document sparkler exit photos from weddings you supplied
- Track reorder patterns and proactively suggest restocking

### Phase 3: Post-Season Relationship Building (October - December)

**Goal:** Convert seasonal accounts to annual contracts. Prepare for next year.

- Send season recap to all accounts (total orders, events supplied)
- Propose annual contracts with volume discounts for next year
- Holiday touchpoint (handwritten note, small gift, or card)
- Gather feedback: what worked, what could improve
- Start researching next year's Dream 25 expansion targets

## Key Timing Insights for Outreach

1. **The March 1 window is non-negotiable.** Planners who haven't heard from you by March aren't hearing from you at all for this season.

2. **Don't cold email in June.** Planners are buried in weddings. Save new outreach for the shoulder months (March-April, September-October).

3. **Annual contracts close in October-November.** That's when planners evaluate who performed well and who gets next year's business.

4. **Engagement season (Nov-Feb) drives next year's volume.** More proposals = more weddings = more sparkler orders 6-12 months later.

5. **Wedding inventory multiplier: 2.5x standard orders.** Wedding season demand is 2.5 times the baseline. Stock up early — running out mid-season damages relationships.
