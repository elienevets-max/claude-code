## 1. Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### The Founder's Triangle: Skill → Market → Leverage

The framework for evaluating whether a business idea is a million-dollar idea. Three questions, each a vertex of the triangle:

| Vertex | Question | What It Means |
|--------|----------|--------------|
| **Domain** | Are you an expert in some domain? | 5+ years in a specific industry. You know the nuances, pain points, buying process, customers, partners, channels. You start at year five; competitors start at zero. |
| **Depth** | Do you have depth in your craft? | "What feels like play to you that feels like work to others?" Could be coding, math, accounting, gardening, piano tuning — any skill where you have genuine depth. |
| **Distribution** | Do you have a unique distribution advantage? | An unfair pathway to reach customers — captive audience, network, partnership, platform, existing relationships. |

**Decision rule:**
- At least one vertex green → you're a go
- All three green → floor it. You're on an accelerated path.

**Case Study — Harvey AI ($8B valuation):**

| Vertex | How They Hit It |
|--------|----------------|
| Domain | Founder Winston Weinberg was a litigator himself — lived the grind |
| Depth | Co-founded with an AI expert from DeepMind and Meta |
| Distribution | Piloted inside top law firms, partnered with some to train their model, expanded across firms they already knew |

All three green. Result: reportedly $8 billion by 2025.

### The DREAM Framework: Five Functions of the Business Machine

Every business runs on five core functions. In the AI era, a solo founder can operate all five with AI augmentation.

| Letter | Function | What It Covers | AI Application Example |
|--------|----------|---------------|----------------------|
| **D** | Demand | Creating a qualified pipeline of leads. How do customers find you? Your lead gen engine. | Clay for enriching 100 leads automatically |
| **R** | Revenue | Pricing, packaging, renewal mechanics, profit margins. Must be crystal clear. | AI pricing analysis from raw material cost data |
| **E** | Engine | Core product or service. Design, engineering, features. What customers actually buy. | Two AI agents: one writes code, one reviews and debugs — shipping features overnight |
| **A** | Admin | Back office: finance, accounting, legal, billing, contracts. Keeps the business running. | Feed QuickBooks + POS data into Notebook LM → generates podcast briefing for branch managers |
| **M** | Marketing | Reputation and brand through content, communities, connections, case studies. | Gamma for slide decks, AI content generation |

**The Mount Everest Principle:** Don't stare at the summit from base camp. Focus on the 18 inches of snow in front of you.

> "Don't worry about building the entire machinery. Just focus on the first piece of a single component of that machine this week."

**Actionable first step:** Pick ONE area of DREAM. Find a single recurring task within it. Automate it this week. That's your first step up the mountain.

### Three Moats That AI Can't Destroy

The real difficulty in the AI-driven world isn't starting a business — it's staying in business. Three defensive moats:

#### Moat 1: Counterpositioning

Sell your product in a way that attacks the core business model of your competition. They'll see what you're doing but can't respond without cannibalizing their own revenue.

| Company | How They Counterpositioned |
|---------|--------------------------|
| **Netflix** | Monthly subscription + mailed DVDs vs. Blockbuster's rental + late fees model. Blockbuster couldn't match without destroying their core revenue. |
| **Southwest Airlines** | No-frills, point-to-point vs. legacy hub-and-spoke with expensive amenities |
| **Airbnb** | Peer-to-peer accommodation vs. hotel chains' owned-property model |
| **Dollar Shave Club** | Subscription razors vs. Gillette's premium retail markup model |

**Blockbuster's fatal error:** Had the chance to buy Netflix for $50 million. Said no. By 2010, bankrupt. Netflix went from $50M to nearly $500B.

**The key insight:** Your competitor will *see* what you're doing. They just won't be able to respond without destroying themselves.

#### Moat 2: Sticky Habits & High Switching Costs

Make leaving you painful. Make using you automatic.

| Product | Why Switching Is Hard |
|---------|---------------------|
| **Google Search** | Bing is one click away. But Google is a *habit*. |
| **iPhone** | Android exists. But the ecosystem (iMessage, AirDrop, Apple Watch) creates enormous friction to leave. |
| **Uber** | Lyft is right there. But your payment info, ride history, driver ratings are all in Uber. |
| **ChatGPT** | Google's Gemini is built into Chrome. People still go to ChatGPT because it's the habit. |

**The question to ask:** How do you make your product a habit? What makes leaving you painful?

#### Moat 3: Proprietary Data & Learning Loops

The most powerful moat. A self-reinforcing cycle where usage generates data, data improves the product, and improved product drives more usage.

```
More Users → More Data → Better Product → More Users → More Data → ...
```

| Company | Their Data Moat |
|---------|----------------|
| **Google** | Search queries refine results, which drive more searches |
| **Amazon** | Purchase data refines recommendations, which drive more purchases |
| **Tesla** | Driving data trains self-driving models, which attract more drivers |
| **Cursor** | Tracks keystrokes from millions of developers → launches a new feature every single day → drives more usage |

> "I'd recommend that you think about this moat the hardest. Is there any proprietary data that you have? How do you get it? Is there a partnership you can strike?"

You don't need Google-scale data. But you need *some* proprietary data that creates a learning loop your competitors can't replicate.

## 2. Actionable Strategies

### The 1000x Cost Collapse Test

Before starting anything, apply this lens:

| Metric | 2 Years Ago | Today with AI |
|--------|------------|--------------|
| Analyzing 1,000 customer reviews | $3,000 + days of work | $3 + minutes |
| Speed | Human speed | Light speed |
| Quality | Limited by human attention | Better precision across vast data |

**What this means:** AI is cheaper, faster, and better than humans at information processing tasks. Any business built on manual information processing is vulnerable. Any business that leverages this collapse has a massive advantage.

### The One-Step-This-Week Method

Don't build the entire DREAM machine at once:

1. Pick one letter: D, R, E, A, or M
2. Identify one recurring task within that function
3. Find one AI tool that can automate or accelerate that task
4. Implement it this week

**Starter suggestions:**
- **Demand:** Use Clay to enrich 100 leads
- **Marketing:** Use Gamma to build a slide deck
- **Engine:** Use AI coding agents (one writes, one reviews) to ship features overnight
- **Admin:** Feed financial data into Notebook LM for automated analysis

### AI Agent Teams for Software Companies

> "Your team can be two AI agents building the next version of your app. One agent writes the code, the other reviews it and debugs it. They could be up all night catching and killing bugs and shipping features."

This is not theoretical — it's the current state of AI tooling. Solo founders can ship software at a pace that previously required teams.

## 3. Failure Patterns & Warnings

### Mistake #1: Ignoring AI as the New Competitive Baseline

Intelligence is now a commodity. The cost of analysis, coding, content creation, and data processing has collapsed by 1000x. If you're building a business that doesn't account for this, you're building on quicksand.

> "In this new high-stakes battlefield, your competition is no longer just another founder or another company. It's the fact that intelligence itself has become a commodity."

### Mistake #2: Starting Without the Founder's Triangle

Building without domain expertise, craft depth, *or* distribution advantage. Having zero vertices green means you're competing with no structural advantage in a world where AI has leveled the basic playing field.

### Mistake #3: Building Without a Moat (The Blockbuster Trap)

Blockbuster had 9,000 stores and $6 billion in revenue. They still went bankrupt because they had no moat against a counterpositioned competitor. Size and revenue alone don't protect you.

**The specific trap:** Making most of your money from something your customers hate (late fees, hidden charges, lock-in). This creates a massive surface area for a counterpositioned competitor.

### Mistake #4: Trying to Build the Whole Machine at Once

Overwhelm kills momentum. The DREAM framework has five functions. Trying to operate all five manually from day one leads to paralysis.

> "You want to reach the top of Mount Everest, but you don't get there by staring at the summit from base camp."

### Mistake #5: Listening to the Inner Critic

> "If you keep listening to that voice in your head, that inner critic of yours, it will always force you to pick regret over risk."

The risks you take and fail at have much less impact than the risks you fail to take.

## 4. Key Insights & Principles

### Intelligence as Commodity

The defining shift of the AI era: intelligence itself is no longer scarce. What's still scarce (and therefore valuable):
- **Taste** — knowing what's good, what resonates, what matters
- **Purpose** — why you're building what you're building
- **Relationships** — trust, networks, human connection
- **Judgment** — context-dependent decision-making
- **Critical thinking** — questioning assumptions, seeing through noise

These are the things AI cannot replicate or automate. This is what you bring to the table.

### The Regret Minimization Test

When staring down risk, ask:

> "When I am on my deathbed looking back, what will I regret most?"

This is the same framework Jeff Bezos famously used to decide to leave D.E. Shaw and start Amazon. The asymmetry is real: failed risks fade. Risks not taken haunt.

> "The risks you take and fail have much less impact than the risks you fail to take."

### Today's AI Is the Worst You'll Ever Use

Whatever AI can do today, it will do better tomorrow. This has two implications:
1. **Start using AI now** — don't wait for it to be "good enough"
2. **Build with AI at the core** — not as an add-on, but as the operating system of your business

### The 58% Benchmark

According to the US Chamber of Commerce, 58% of small businesses are already using generative AI. If you're in the other 42%, you're falling behind the baseline, not competing at the frontier.

## 5. Contextual Applications

### When the Founder's Triangle Applies Best

- **Before committing to an idea** — use it as a go/no-go filter
- **When choosing between multiple ideas** — pick the one with more green vertices
- **When forming a founding team** — look for co-founders who fill missing vertices (Harvey AI: domain expert + AI depth expert)
- **When pivoting** — reassess which vertices are still green in the new direction

### When DREAM Applies Best

- **Solo founders** overwhelmed by wearing all hats — pick one letter, automate one task
- **Early-stage startups** trying to systematize — build each function with AI from the start
- **Existing small businesses** looking to modernize — audit which of the five functions is most manual and start there

### When Moats Matter Most

- **After finding product-market fit** — you've validated demand, now defend the territory
- **When competitors appear** — the moat question becomes urgent
- **When pricing is being commoditized** — if AI makes your core product cheap, your moat is all that's left

### Industry-Specific Applications

| Industry | Moat Strategy |
|----------|--------------|
| **Legal tech** (Harvey AI model) | Domain expertise + proprietary training data from law firm partnerships |
| **Software/Dev tools** (Cursor model) | Keystroke/usage data → daily feature launches → self-reinforcing loop |
| **Local services** | Counterpositioning against manual competitors with AI-powered pricing, scheduling, communication |
| **E-commerce** | Customer behavior data → personalized recommendations → higher conversion → more data |

## 6. Questions Raised

### Unresolved Tensions

1. **If AI makes starting easier, does it make defending harder?** The same tools that let you launch in a weekend let your competitor do the same. Do moats matter more than ever, or are they harder to build than ever?

2. **Domain expertise vs. AI disruption:** If you have 5 years of domain expertise but AI can learn the domain in minutes, how durable is the domain vertex of the Founder's Triangle? The answer likely lies in *tacit* knowledge — relationships, political dynamics, trust — that AI can't absorb from data.

3. **Counterpositioning lifespan:** Netflix counterpositioned against Blockbuster, but now faces its own counterpositioning from free content on YouTube and TikTok. Is counterpositioning a permanent moat or a temporary advantage?

4. **Solo founder ceiling:** The DREAM framework assumes AI can replace team members across all five functions. At what scale does this break? When do you need humans, and for which functions first?

5. **Data moat accessibility:** Cursor can track millions of keystrokes because developers use their product. But what if you're starting from zero users? How do you build a data moat when you have no data? The bootstrapping paradox of AI-era businesses.

6. **The "worst AI you'll ever use" paradox:** If today's AI is the worst, should you build on current capabilities or bet on future ones? Building for today's AI means potential rework. Building for tomorrow's means building on speculation.
