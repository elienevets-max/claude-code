## The Language of Money

Most people do not have the equivalent of a driver's license when it comes to money. Understanding a few key numbers is like understanding the speed limit — once you know them, everything else follows.

**The basic language of money:**
- What percentage of your income are you saving, and why?
- What percentage are you investing, and why?
- When will you reach $100K, $500K, or $1M — and what will that money get you?

> Having a million dollars in the bank is pointless. What does it get you specifically?

You need to know your key 4-6 numbers. Not many — just a few. But those numbers tell a story about your priorities, your alignment, and your future.

## The Conscious Spending Plan

Four categories that should account for 100% of your take-home pay:

| Category | % of Take-Home | What It Covers |
|----------|---------------|----------------|
| **Fixed Costs** | 50–60% | Rent/mortgage, debt payments, groceries, car, utilities — everything essentially fixed each month |
| **Savings** | 5–10% | Emergency fund, down payment savings, specific goal-based savings |
| **Investments** | 5–10% | Long-term wealth building — this is where real wealth is created |
| **Guilt-Free Spending** | 20–35% | Cocktails, clothes, treating friends, yoga, whatever you love |

### How to Use This

1. Take 15 minutes and jot down your approximate numbers — back of the napkin
2. Benchmark your actual spending against these percentages
3. Look for incongruities between what you claim your rich life is and how you actually spend

**What these numbers reveal:** Show someone your four numbers and they can tell what you love spending on, what you don't, what your priorities are, and where you are out of alignment.

### The Alignment Test

If someone says "I want freedom" but has a 30-year mortgage, car payments, and debt payments anchoring them down — that's a dissonance worth examining. Your spending should reflect your stated values.

## Investing Fundamentals

### Where Real Wealth Is Created

Investing is not for rich people after 40. One of the ways you **get** rich is by investing. You can start with any amount.

### The Simplest Investment Strategy

**Get a target date fund.** One fund. That's it.

1. **Pick the fund** based on the year you'll retire (e.g., if you'll be 65 in 2055, pick a "2055 fund")
2. **Open an account** at a low-cost brokerage — Vanguard, Schwab, or Fidelity
3. **Set up automatic monthly transfers** from your checking account
4. **Never touch it**

### What Is a Fund?

A fund is a basket of stocks (and maybe bonds). It owns hundreds of companies — Microsoft, Google, and many more. You don't need to pick 20 individual stocks. The fund does the diversification for you automatically.

A target date fund also gets more conservative as you age. Someone who's 25 should invest differently than someone who's 75 — the fund handles this shift automatically.

### The 5% Starting Point

> "Anyone who comes to me and says there's no way they can invest — show me where you're spending your money. I guarantee I can find 5% to send in every month."

You're not trying to invest. It's a habit. Investing should be even easier than brushing your teeth because you set it up once and it runs automatically.

### How to Treat Your Investment Account

- **It is NOT a checking account** — do not withdraw from it
- **Do not check it daily** — no investing apps on your phone
- **Check every 3-6 months** — log in, see if it's up or down, log out
- **Do not fiddle with it** — once the turkey is in the oven, let it cook for decades

> "Making Thanksgiving dinner — once you've put the turkey in the oven, just let it sit. Do not fiddle with it because you're only going to mess it up. And in this case, you're letting the turkey cook for decades."

## The Power of Compound Interest

### Worked Example: Starting at 16

| Scenario | Starting Amount | Annual Investment | Years | Return | Result |
|----------|----------------|-------------------|-------|--------|--------|
| Investing to age 30 | $5,000 | $5,000/yr (~$400/mo) | 14 | 7% | **$133,537** |
| Investing to age 40 | $5,000 | $5,000/yr | 24 | 7% | **$336,000** |
| Investing to age 50 | $5,000 | $5,000/yr | 34 | 7% | **$736,000** |
| Investing to age 65 (income grows) | $5,000 | $30,000/yr avg | 49 | 7% | **$12,330,000** |

Key assumptions: As income rises through your 30s and 40s, your investment amount increases. The $30K/year average accounts for investing little early on and much more later.

### Warren Buffett and Time

Over 99% of Warren Buffett's wealth was accumulated after age 60. Not because of a fancy strategy — because he started investing at a very young age and let compounding do its work for 60+ years.

### Why 7%?

The stock market has returned roughly 10-11% per year over the last ~100 years. Adjusted for inflation, that's about 7-8%. Using 7% is conservative — if you get 8%, great, but don't count on it.

> "I never want to be surprised on the downside. If anything, I'm going to make more."

### The Counterintuitive Nature of Investing

In most areas of life, paying more gets you better results. $100 sushi is better than $20 sushi. An expensive sweater has better fabric.

**Investing is the opposite.** If you spend more (in fees), you don't get better returns. You get *worse* returns. This is profoundly counterintuitive, but in investing, costs matter enormously.

## The Hidden Cost of Fees

A financial adviser charging 1% doesn't sound like much. But over a lifetime, that 1% fee takes **28% of your total returns**.

| Fee | Lifetime Return Lost |
|-----|---------------------|
| 1% | ~28% of your returns |
| 2% | ~55% of your returns |

If you make $1M in investment returns over your life, $280,000 goes straight to the advisor's pocket at a 1% fee.

**How to verify:** Search "investment cost calculator" online, plug in your numbers with a 1% fee, and watch what happens.

**The takeaway:** Keep your costs low. Use low-cost index funds and target date funds.

## Why Buying a House Can Be a Bad Investment

### The Popular Narrative

The American/Western dream: own a single-family home with a white picket fence. Owning = successful. Renting = loser. This is the result of decades of messaging — some might call it propaganda.

### The Grandma Math Problem

> Grandma bought a house in Austin, Texas in 1970 for $100,000. She sold it 50 years later for $1,000,000. Grandma made $900,000!

**What Grandma didn't factor in:**
- Maintenance costs over 50 years
- Inflation and its effect on returns
- Opportunity cost of the down payment invested in the S&P 500
- Phantom costs: interest on the loan, property taxes, insurance
- Transaction costs of buying and selling

It's not simply bigger number minus smaller number. That's wrong. That's simplistic.

### The Real Numbers

Over approximately 100 years of data, housing has essentially matched inflation — slightly above. Compare that to the S&P 500's ~7% real return.

### Rent vs. Own: A Real Example

Living in New York, the same apartment would cost **2.2x more to own than to rent**:

| | Monthly Cost |
|---|---|
| Renting | $3,000 |
| Owning (with phantom costs) | $6,400 |
| **Difference invested monthly** | **$3,400** |

By renting and investing the $3,400 difference, you make more money than you would by owning.

### Phantom Costs of Ownership

When calculating the true cost of owning, add 50% to the mortgage payment to account for:
- Roof repairs and maintenance (amortized over time)
- Property taxes
- Insurance
- Interest on the loan
- Labor costs for repairs
- Transaction costs when buying/selling

### The Flexibility Cost

Buying a house is one of the most profound financial decisions affecting your **lifestyle**:
- Selling a house involves massive transaction costs and labor
- You can sell a car at a minor loss, but a house is a different story
- If you're not sure you'll be in the same place for **at least 10 years**, buying may not make sense
- Spreading transaction costs over 10+ years makes them affordable — like wearing an expensive jacket once vs. for 10 years

### The Key Message

> "I've been accused of saying buying a house is bad. No, I never said that. In fact, I will buy a house myself one day. And when I do, it's going to be a terrible financial decision. And I'm going to do it anyway."

- Buying **can** be a good financial decision
- Renting **can** be a good financial decision
- For the biggest purchase of your life, **run the numbers**
- Never feel guilty for renting
- 75%+ of home buyers have never created a simple spreadsheet comparing the costs

### Real Estate as Investment

Owning real estate as a rental investment can work as part of a diversified portfolio — **if you run the numbers**. Remember: if the mortgage is $1,000/month, budget $1,500/month (add 50%) to account for all real costs. If it cash flows after that, it's a legitimate investment.

## Crypto and Speculative Investing

### The Position

If you have a well-diversified portfolio and want to have fun with **1-5% of your portfolio**, go ahead — crypto, individual stocks, your friend's bar in Brooklyn, whatever.

### The Warning Signs

People attracted to crypto tend to be extremely risk-seeking and view diversification as "boring for old people."

**Red flags:**
- Putting 100% of your portfolio in a single speculative asset
- Believing 4,000% annual returns are normal when the stock market returns ~7%/year
- Seeing risk management as weakness rather than wisdom

### The Asymmetry of Sharing

> "We love to share our successes. You almost never hear anyone saying they lost 85% of their money."

When crypto was booming, everyone was vocal. When it crashed, fewer than 3 responses came from a large social media following when asked to share loss stories. Sharing losses is deeply shameful — it's the opposite of status.

## Increasing Your Income

### The Personal Trainer Example

Starting point: Charging $100/hour, clients stay ~3 months, making ~$60K/year.

**Three levers to double income:**

1. **Find more clients** — Ask existing clients for referrals. "I've got a few slots available. Who would you recommend?"

2. **Increase revenue per client** — Listen to what else clients need:
   - Package meal planning services (+$200/month per client)
   - Partner with food delivery services for macro-matched meals (take a cut)
   - Run free group sessions on Saturdays to find new clients

3. **Increase client duration** — Offer a 6-month plan at a small discount ($95/session instead of $100) to lock in longer relationships

### Placing Your Skills in the Right Market

The same skill set yields dramatically different returns depending on the market:

| Same Skill | Low-Value Market | High-Value Market |
|-----------|-----------------|------------------|
| Graphic design | Nightclub flyers ($50-100/project) | Luxury brand design in Dubai ($50,000/project) |
| Marketing | Consumer goods (standard salary) | Biotech going public ($8M in options for 9 months) |
| Writing | Blog posts | Scientific/biotech writing (5x the pay) |

> A company listed on the London Stock Exchange might get 4x revenue valuation. The exact same company on the New York Stock Exchange: 8x revenue. Same company, same people, same business — different market.

### Think in Discontinuous Jumps

Most people think linearly: "I make $100/month, what if I made $120?" But real wealth comes from discontinuous jumps — $100 to $5,000. Something big has to change: moving markets, developing a new skill, partnering, specializing.

In investing, the discontinuous jump comes from **time** — and the human mind is not built to intuitively grasp compounding.

## Account Structure and Automation

### The System (Set Up Once, Never Think About It Again)

```
Paycheck
  → Checking Account
      → Auto-transfer to Savings Account
          → Sub-accounts: Emergency, Vacation, Car, Down Payment
      → Auto-transfer to Investment Account
          → Target date fund (don't touch this)
      → Guilt-free spending
      → Credit card (auto-paid in full every month)
```

- Takes a couple of weeks to set up
- Then you never have to think about it again
- Spend less than 1 hour per month on finances after setup
- The investment fund automatically draws from your checking account each month

> "I don't try to brush my teeth in the morning. It's a habit. Investing is even easier than brushing your teeth because you set it up automatically."
